# Game Corner

Multiplayer party games for family and friends. The host creates a room and shares a link; guests join with a name.
Games: Tic-Tac-Toe, Connect 4, Trivia (4 question packs + your own), Bingo, Rock Paper Scissors, Memory Match, Draw & Guess, Impostor, Hangman, Word Race, Charades.

Bingo: players shuffle their own cards (always unique) until the host starts. The host can give players extra cards (everyone sees the card counts), pick win patterns from a library of 38 shapes (classic lines, letters A–Z shapes, hearts, stars, a Christmas tree, a house, a smiley and more), choose one or several (a card wins with any one of them) or all, or draw a custom one square by square, and choose how numbers are drawn: instant (everyone sees it) or private roll (only the host sees it until they reveal it). Wins are detected automatically and the winning cells light up gold; every revealed number plays an animated ball draw. The host can end a game early or start a new one, and every finished game goes into a history (winners, pattern, numbers called, games played, wins per player) that lasts for the whole room session.

Names: everyone sees their own name at the top of their screen. If two players pick the same name, the later joiner gets a stable 5-digit ID, like "Nicole (31351)" (and is told why), so the host can tell who won.

Players: everyone sees a live total at the top of their screen ("👥 7 players", or "👥 7 / 10 players · 3 spots left" when the host sets a limit). The host can limit how many people can join (the host counts as one), or choose no limit. When the room is full, newcomers see a "room is full" message with a Try again button; players already in are never removed, someone who drops out keeps their seat for 5 minutes, and the host can free held seats at any time. The limit is enforced by the host's browser.

People who join during a game: the host chooses in Room settings. Automatic (the default) makes newcomers watch Bingo until the next game, because a latecomer's card would start with every called number marked, while the casual games let them join in. The host can also force Always watch or Always join in, and let watchers in at any time. Everyone who was there when the game began, including someone who only dropped out for a moment, keeps playing. Watchers see the game, are counted in the total ("👀 2 watching"), and can't act; the host's browser ignores their moves.

Secrets: anything private to one player (your role, your word, a charades prompt) is encrypted for that player only. Everyone joins with a fresh key pair; the host seals each private message with a key shared with its recipient, so other players looking at the network traffic see only scrambled bytes, and the host's own secrets never leave the host's phone. (The host's phone still sees every secret, as it runs the game.)

Dropped connections: the host's game keeps every number that is called, so a player who drops out and comes back sees their card filled in exactly as if they never left. In Bingo a player who drops out stays in the game for 5 minutes and can still win while offline (the host and everyone see "was offline"); a returning phone asks the host for the current game straight away.

Phones: built for phones first. Big tap targets, no sideways scrolling, no iPhone zoom on inputs, safe-area padding, and touch drawing. The screen is kept awake while you are in a room (the host's phone runs the game, so if it sleeps the game would freeze), the invite button opens the phone's share sheet, and the room re-syncs when a phone wakes up.

Host tools: pick a game and its options (question pack, word difficulty, prompt type), remove players, close the room. Refreshing a tab rejoins the same room (guests) or resumes the room and game (host). Sound effects have a mute button.

- **Frontend:** static files (no build step), hosted on any static host.
- **Backend:** Supabase (Auth, Postgres for rooms, Realtime for gameplay).
- **Schema:** `supabase/migration.sql`. Enable "Allow anonymous sign-ins" in Supabase Auth settings.
- **Add a game:** create `games/<name>.js` exporting `{ id, name, emoji, init(env, opts), reduce(state, action, env), render(el, state, ui) }` and list it in `games/index.js`.
  Optional hooks: `view(state)` (public copy that hides secrets), `priv(state)` (per-player secrets, e.g. roles), `deadline(state)` (timestamp; the host fires a `timeout` action then).
- **How it works:** the host's browser runs the game rules and broadcasts state over a Supabase Realtime channel. Guests send actions; the host applies them. Moves are trusted from each browser, which is fine for family play but not for competitive use.

Home screen: after signing in you get a "Start a game night" card (create a room, or join a friend's with a code) and every game as a card: a one-line pitch, players (e.g. 5+), typical length, type (Party, Classic, Words & quiz, Draw & act), a Kid-friendly badge, and How to play. Filter by how many are playing and by type. The details live in `games/meta.js`; a new game must be added there too (the tests check it).

Privacy and terms: `privacy.html` and `terms.html` are plain pages served at `/privacy` and `/terms` (`vercel.json` turns on clean URLs). Every home screen links to them in the footer, and the sign-in form links to them too, as Google's OAuth branding check requires. Update them if the app starts collecting anything new.

Landing page: signed-out visitors see a landing page with three games as a teaser (the rest are locked), how it works, the sign-in form and a "Got a room code?" box. After signing in, the home screen lists every game with its goal and how to play. Guests with an invite link or room code still join without an account.

Accounts: anyone can sign up and host rooms, with **Continue with Google** or with an email and a password (8+ characters). A new email account gets a 6-digit code by email and must enter it (or tap the link in the email) before it can sign in, so only real addresses get in. Guests joining a room still don't need an account.

Supabase setup for sign-in:
1. **Authentication → Sign In / Providers → Email:** turn on "Confirm email". Keep "Allow anonymous sign-ins" on for guests.
2. **Authentication → Emails → Confirm signup:** put the code in the email, e.g. `<p>Your Game Corner code is <b>{{ .Token }}</b></p><p>Or <a href="{{ .ConfirmationURL }}">confirm your email</a>.</p>`. Set up custom SMTP for real traffic, as Supabase's built-in mailer only sends a few emails an hour.
3. **Authentication → Sign In / Providers → Google:** turn on and paste the Client ID and secret from a Google Cloud OAuth client (type "Web application") whose authorized redirect URI is `https://<project>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback`.
4. **Authentication → URL Configuration:** Site URL `https://gamecorner.io`, and add it (plus `http://localhost:3000` for local testing) to the redirect URLs.
5. Existing database: run `supabase/002_open_hosting.sql` once in the SQL editor so every verified account can host (before, only the first account could).

Run locally: `npx serve .`

## Tests

`npm test` checks every game's rules, scoring, secret-info handling and timers (no browser needed).

## Werewolf

Needs 5+ players. The host picks who runs the game:

- **No moderator** – the app runs everything on timers (night, discussion, vote); the host plays too.
- **Host moderates** – the host sees every role, reads the script and presses ▶ to move on (does not play).
- **A player moderates** – the host picks one player to moderate; the host plays.

Roles: Werewolf, Villager, Seer, Doctor, optional Hunter. Wolves count is automatic or fixed; the host can toggle roles, whether the dead's roles are shown, and whether the dead can see everyone's roles. Roles and night actions are sent encrypted to each player.

Werewolf atmosphere: the night screen has a moonlit sky with a howling wolf, bats and twinkling stars, crickets and wind, a far-off howl, and a heartbeat in the last 10 seconds. Each morning every phone plays the same short scene (tap to skip): a wolf lunges out of the dark and either claws the victim's name or is driven back. The scene is identical on every device and the night-action buttons all make the same quiet sound, so nothing gives away who the wolves are. Respects the sound mute button and "reduce motion".

**Face-to-face mode** (Werewolf option): when everyone sits in the same room, tapping on a phone at night gives wolves away. With this on, every player gets the identical night screen and must pick someone. A wolf's pick is the kill vote, the seer's pick is inspected (answer arrives at dawn), the doctor's pick is protected, and everyone else's pick is a decoy. A "peek" button explains what your own pick does. Players who skip the night can't vote the next day.

Villagers and the hunter get a decoy job at night in normal mode too (a seer-style or doctor-style screen whose pick does nothing, never sent to anyone), so an idle phone doesn't give them away.

### Werewolf: more roles and rules
- **Roles:** 🛡️ Bodyguard (dies in place of the person they guard), 🧙‍♀️ Witch (one heal and one poison per game; sees who the wolves chose once the pack has decided), 💘 Cupid (picks two lovers on night one; they die together and win together if they are the last two on opposite sides), 🎭 Tanner (wins alone if voted out). Witch and Cupid are off in face-to-face mode (they need two choices).
- **Voting:** optional open voting (votes visible live) and a runoff vote on a tie. Votes are read out one by one with a count-up and a farewell for the player who is out.
- **Atmosphere:** sunrise and dusk themes, a daytime sky with birds, glowing wolf eyes on a wolf's own night screen, a golden shield scene for the doctor when a save works, a hunter's bang, and a ghost view with a timeline for players who are out.
- **After the game:** a night-by-night recap of what everyone did and a leaderboard (wins, games, survived) that carries across rematches.
- **Moderator tools:** a read-aloud script (one line per step, with a sound), and a countdown the moderator can start, extend or stop. When it runs out the game moves on.

Werewolf vote privacy: the host can turn **Show who voted for whom** off in the lobby. The result then shows only the totals (and the number of skips), and the individual votes are never sent to players.

### Werewolf: pause and drop-outs
- **Pause:** the host or moderator can pause the game (bathroom break!). Everyone sees a "paused" banner, countdowns freeze, nothing can be played, and when they resume the timers pick up where they stopped.
- **Someone goes offline:** a 🔌 shows next to their name. After 2 minutes the host (or moderator) is asked, without naming any roles: **Wait**, **Autopilot** (the app plays random legal moves for them and they take over again if they return), or **Remove** (they are out as "left the game"; if that was the last wolf the village wins). People who are offline that long are no longer waited for when closing the night or a vote. If the **moderator** drops, the host can switch the game to timers.

### Room bar and menu
A fixed bar stays at the top of every screen with the room code, the 🔊 sound switch (for everyone) and a ⚙ menu. The host's menu holds every host control: invite link, back to the game list, player limit, what happens when people join mid-game, and close room. Other players' menu has Leave room. A paused Werewolf game covers the whole screen for everyone, wherever they have scrolled to.

Werewolf in face-to-face mode: at dawn and during the day every living player has the same "Peek at my night result" button (the seer sees who they inspected, the doctor sees a successful save, everyone else sees "nothing to report").

With a moderator (host or player), Werewolf no longer moves on by itself: when everyone has acted at night, everyone has voted, or most players are ready to vote, the moderator gets a green "everyone is done" prompt with a beep, the big button glows, and players see "Waiting for the moderator…". The moderator then presses the button when ready. Without a moderator the game still moves on by itself.

### Impostor: animation and sound
Role cards flip in with a whoosh (the impostor's card pulses red with a growl and heartbeat; crew get a soft ding and see their word in big letters). Turn-order chips show who has given a clue, with a "Your turn!" banner (blip and vibration) and animated "…is thinking" dots for everyone else. New clues pop in, the vote screen has a progress bar and ✅/⏳ chips with a tick for each vote, a caught impostor triggers an alarm banner and drum roll, and the result is revealed beat by beat: votes read out one at a time, "The impostor was…", the name, the secret word, then the verdict with confetti and a win or lose jingle for your side.

Impostor result scene: when a round ends, every phone plays a full-screen reveal (tap to skip). A spotlight sweeps a dark screen with floating question marks, the detective runs in with a magnifier, a drum roll builds into "The impostor was…", the name slams in with a flash and screen shake, the secret word flips over (with who was voted out and any final guess), and the verdict lands with confetti and a win or lose jingle for your side. Reduced-motion settings skip the picture and keep the sounds.

Impostor clue round: at the start of every turn each phone shows a short full-screen banner (tap to skip): "YOUR TURN!" with a bouncing mic, a blip-blip-ding and a vibration for the player whose turn it is, and "<name>'s turn · Listen to their clue" for everyone else, with a "Clue 2 of 4" counter.

Impostor scores: right after the verdict, the full-screen scene switches to a 🏆 Scores board. Rows slide in one by one (👑 for the leader, your own name highlighted), each bar grows, each total counts up from its old score to the new one with a tick, and a green "+1" or "+2" pops next to anyone who scored this round. The same +points badges appear on the result page afterwards.

### Impostor: categories and score card
- **Categories:** the host now opens a waiting room first and chooses what the secret words are about: tap any of the 11 categories (🍕 Food, 🦁 Animals, 🏖️ Places, …) or leave it on 🎲 Everything. The word is picked at random only from those categories, and a word is not repeated until its category runs out. Everyone sees the choice; only the host can change it, in the waiting room or between rounds (the choice is remembered when you switch games). The Start button stays pinned at the bottom of the screen.
- **Score card:** the scoreboard is now a podium for the top three (gold, silver and bronze pedestals, coloured initial avatars, a crown for first place) with a ranked list below and green +points badges. It appears on the result page and builds up in the full-screen scores scene: pedestals rise, avatars drop in, totals count up.

Impostor screens are now made of separate cards instead of one long card: a purple header strip (round and category), your role card, and a card per step (clues, vote, last chance). The result page is a big green or red verdict banner, a card with the impostor's avatar next to the secret word and its category, a "Who voted for whom" card grouped by the person voted for (voter chips, bars, vote counts, "voted out" and "impostor" tags), a "Last chance" card with the final guess, and the podium scoreboard.

Impostor privacy: the secret word (or "you are the impostor") is hidden at the start of every round behind a "👁 Tap to see your secret" card that looks identical for everyone. Tapping reveals it, "🙈 Hide my secret" puts it away, and it hides itself after 10 seconds. The sounds and vibration at the start of a round are the same for everyone, so a phone's speaker can't give the impostor away.

## 🎁 Bunutan (secret gift exchange)
- **Waiting room:** everyone who opens the host's link joins the draw, and the host watches the list fill up. The host can include or leave themselves out of the draw, set the event name, gift budget, date and a note, choose "no swaps", and keep chosen pairs (like partners) from drawing each other. The "Draw the names" button stays pinned at the bottom.
- **The draw:** everyone sees the names being mixed into a gift box, then each person taps their own gift, watches the names spin on a reel that slows and lands on theirs, and sees it with confetti. Nobody can draw themselves, and nobody else sees who you drew: each result is sent encrypted to that one person.
- **Coming back:** the name stays theirs. After a refresh or leaving and returning on the same device, "Tap to see who you drew" shows the same name (it hides again after a few seconds, so nobody nearby can read it).
- **Save as image:** a festive 1080×1350 picture with the event, the name, budget and date, with two separate buttons: 📤 Share (opens the phone's share sheet, shown only when the device supports sharing files) and 📥 Download (saves the PNG to the device). You can also long-press the picture.
- **Even after the host leaves:** each person's draw is also saved on their own phone (browser storage). If the host closes the room or it no longer exists, the "room closed" / "not found" screen lists "Saved on this device" so they can still open their gift (the opening animation plays the first time) and see the name, hidden until tapped. It can be saved as an image there too, or removed from the device.
- **Host sees who saved:** the progress list shows 📥 / 📤 next to people who downloaded or shared their picture, plus "N/M saved their picture". It only knows about the Share and Download buttons (not long-press saves or screenshots), and never sees the name.
- **Progress:** everyone can see how many people have drawn (names only, never who drew whom). The host can start a new draw with the same details.
- **Limits:** the host's phone computes the draw, so the host could in principle read it with developer tools; choose "Include me" off for a perfectly neutral host. Someone who clears their browser data or changes device can't get back to their name (the keepsake lives only on that phone), which is why the picture is offered.

## 💰 Secret Millionaire (hidden millionaire, team tasks, clues)
- **Who moderates (like Werewolf):** the host picks one of three modes. *The host moderates*: the host does not play, knows who the Millionaire is, picks the secrets and steers the agenda. *Another player moderates*: the host plays and a chosen player is the moderator (the host keeps the progress buttons as a safety net, but cannot see the secrets). *No moderator*: the app runs on timers, everyone plays (the host too), nobody can see the secrets. Players need 4+ besides the moderator.
- **When someone's phone drops out:** they show a 🔌 for everyone. The game does not wait for them: a round ends when everybody who is still here has finished every room, and the final vote when everybody who is still here has voted. They pick up where they left off when they come back (their points, team and secrets are kept). If the moderator is gone for 20+ seconds the host is nudged to hand over (a random player is the safe choice).
- **Handing over mid-game:** the moderator (or the host, as a safety net if the moderator steps away) can hand moderating to another player, back to the host, or to nobody (the app then runs on timers). The new moderator stops playing and the previous one joins the players, on the smaller team. The moderator can pick a named player but never the Millionaire. The host, who may not know who that is, can only choose "a random player", "I'll moderate" or "nobody", so the choice can't give the Millionaire away.
- **Role cards:** at the start every player gets the same "your secret role is ready" screen, then taps to reveal: gold and "SECRET MILLIONAIRE" with the agenda, or blue "detective". The card stays hidden until tapped, hides again after a few seconds, and looks identical for everyone while hidden, so people sitting next to you can't tell. The moderator's secrets are also hidden until tapped.
- **Timers:** choose the time per round (or "no limit": the moderator ends it), the break between rounds (wait for the moderator, or the next round starts after 15–180 s) and the final-vote time. The moderator (and the host) can end a round any time, add 30 s or 1 min, start a countdown, or remove the limit. With no moderator all clocks stay on.
- **Difficulty:** Easy, Normal, Hard, or *Rising* (default), which gets harder every round. Every task has three levels (e.g. more eggs to catch, longer sequences, a tighter dial, fewer allowed slips). All the numbers live in `games/secret-levels.js`. They were tuned against a model of a typical player (`tests/secret-balance-model.mjs`) so that about 96% / 80% / 62% pass on easy / normal / hard; `npm test` fails if a change pushes a task out of its band. The model is a set of assumptions about people (about 0.37 s reaction, about 6 fast taps a second, a memory span of about 5), not measurements, so the end-of-game screen shows a **task stats** table (tries, pass rate, typical time per task) to adjust the numbers from real games.
- **A round:** players walk a live top-down mansion (drag on the phone, or arrow keys / WASD on a computer; walls and doors stop you) and stand on the glowing pad in a room for a moment: its task opens in a pop-up window on your phone (or tap the button). Each room (Kitchen, Library, Garage, Garden, Vault) has four tasks and one is picked at random each time. Kitchen: pancake reaction, catch the eggs, simmer the soup, order up. Library: book order, odd one out, unscramble, quiz. Garage: tap 1–9, match the wires, pump the tyre, stop the clock. Garden: quick sums, squash the bugs, harvest, water the plants. Vault: stop the dial, crack the code, match the gems, lights out. The moderator can switch to a plain room list (uses less data).
- **Finding your team's clue:** as soon as a round's results are in (after the battle result, if there was a battle), every player on the winning team gets a **full-screen envelope** on their phone: tap to open, read the clue, then **share with everyone** or **keep it secret** (or tap Later). A yellow banner at the top of the results page does the same if you close it. Everyone else sees a short "🔴 found a clue!" banner, and whenever someone shares a clue a banner with its text appears on every phone for a few seconds. The moderator never gets the envelope (they see every clue in their own panel).
- **Clues and sharing:** the winning team (both teams on a tie) gets a private clue made from what really happened. Any holder can **share it with everyone** (it appears for all players and the moderator, with who shared it, and cannot be edited) or **keep it secret** and still share it later. Once one holder shares it, it counts for the whole team. Only holders can share.
- **Checking a player (the 🔍 check):** once per round a player can check someone standing next to them on the live map (or in the same room in the list version) and learn which rooms that person has visited so far this round. Only the checker sees the answer, which stays in their notebook; the person checked is only told that somebody did. Checks are limited by the screen (you must be close), not verified by the host.
- **Secret agenda:** the Millionaire gets one per round (visit or avoid a room, visit two specific rooms, 4+ or 2 or fewer rooms, finish a task fast, fail one, use their check, make their team win or lose, do 3+ or 1 or fewer tasks) and earns a bonus when it's done. The moderator can choose it or leave it random.
- **Accusations and vote:** each player may accuse once between rounds (right = the game ends and they win, wrong = sit out the next round). After the last round everyone votes; if most pick the Millionaire the group splits the $1,000,000 pot, otherwise the Millionaire keeps it plus their bonuses.
- **Full screen and countdowns:** while walking, the mansion fills the whole phone with just a slim bar (round, time, team scores, your progress, a 🎭 button to look at your role again and ☰ for the normal page with everything else). Each round opens with a "Round 2, medium tasks" screen and a 3-2-1-GO on every phone at once, and nobody can move until GO. Each task opens full screen in its room's colours with its own 3-2-1. Short banners announce "Round complete!" and "The final vote".
- **Team battles (Red vs Blue):** after a round's tasks, both teams play the same quick game at the same moment on every phone, full screen, with a 3-2-1. Six games take turns (each is used once before any comes back, in a random order): **Tug of War** (tap PULL, live rope), **Trivia Showdown** (five questions, same for everyone), **Reflex Race** (tap on green, best of three, quicker team wins), **Maths Race** (as many sums as you can in 30 seconds), **Memory Chain** (repeat a pattern that grows by one; one mistake and you are out) and **Gem Rush** (tap falling gems, avoid the bombs). Scores are the **average per player**, so a bigger team has no edge, and a tie gives no bonus. Progress is shared live; each player's final score is one message and impossible scores are refused. The host chooses in the lobby: **how often** (after every round, every second round, now and then at random with the last round always included, only after the last round, or never), **the bonus** (+15, +25, +40 or +60 team points, which can decide who wins the round and gets the clue), whether **the last round's battle is worth double**, and **which of the six games** are in play. A result scene shows both averages, who won and the new totals counting up. The moderator (or the host) can skip a battle; players who are offline are not waited for.
- **Score ticker:** every time a task is finished, the two team totals flash up on EVERY phone (players, the moderator and spectators): both numbers, a "+N" pop on the team that scored with a count-up, and a bar showing who leads. It only shows teams and points, never who scored, so the secret roles stay secret.
- **Look:** the mansion is painted in detail (parquet hall with a runner, tiled kitchen, library with bookshelves and a rug, garage with a car, garden with a fountain and flower beds, a gold vault), with glowing task pads. The characters are little round buddies with a beanie in their team's colour; they bob and swing their feet when walking, blink, look where they go and leave a puff of dust. Reduced-motion settings turn most of this off.
- **Walking speed and data:** the moderator chooses slow, normal or fast. Positions are sent as small messages only while someone moves (about 2–3 a second, plus a slow heartbeat) and are never stored in the game state. Supabase's free plan has message limits, so very large groups may prefer the room list.
- **Limits:** the host's phone computes everything, so a determined host could read the secrets with developer tools, and that includes when another player moderates. Host-only choices are not sent over the shared channel. The task difficulty settings are reasoned guesses, not measured on real players.
